# TF N5: what it's modelled on

The Korg N5 (1998), from its owner's manual: *N1/N5 Owner's Manual*, Chapter 3 "Parameter guide" (Korg).
`Source/N5Model.{h,cpp}` holds every parameter as the manual gives it, `Source/N5Synth.{h,cpp}` is the
engine, `Source/N5Effects.{h,cpp}` the effects, `Source/N5Panel.cpp` the window, `Tests/N5Test.cpp` checks it.

## What's here, and what isn't

| Part of the N5 | Here | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Programs: SINGLE or DOUBLE oscillators, each with its pitch LFO and EG, VDF (and its LFO and EG), VDA (and its LFO and EG), pan and C/D sends; MONO/POLY, portamento, wheel and aftertouch routing | All of it | Every parameter, range and step from the manual (pp. 31-42) |
| 64 voices, 32 notes in DOUBLE | Yes | The oldest released voice goes first, then the oldest (the manual doesn't say which goes) |
| Its 527 multisamples and 285 drum samples (12 MB of Korg's recordings) | No | Simple waves (sine, triangle, saw, square, 25 % and 12 % pulse, noise) and 16 drawn single cycles in their place. The N5's samples aren't Korg's to give away here, and a faithful sound would need them |
| Drumkits (DRUMS oscillator mode, Drumkit Edit) | No | They map drum samples to keys; with no drum samples, left out |
| Combinations: 8 timbres, each with volume, pan, transpose, fine tune, C/D sends, note and velocity windows, and its receive switches | All of it | pp. 28-30. The timbre's pan adds to its program's; its sends replace the program's |
| The two effects units: 48 types, their parameters, initial values and dynamic modulation; SERIAL, PARA.1, PARA.2 and PARA.3 placement; PAN 3/4 and PARA.3's levels | All of it | pp. 45-56: the table of 48 types is reproduced exactly. How each type sounds is ours (below) |
| The arpeggiator: 20 types, octave 1-4, 40-240 BPM, OFF/LATCH/K.SYNC/L&K.S, step 1/4..1/16 T, velocity (value, KEY, STEP), gate (%, STEP), sort, swing | All of it | pp. 24-27. ARP 1-6 and the B- and D- patterns are only named: what they play is ours |
| The realtime knobs' controllers | CC 74, 73, 75, 72 (cutoff, attack, decay, release), 7, 11, 1, 16, 17, 64 | p. 25 |
| Performance mode, Multi mode (16 parts, GM/GS/XG), the factory programs | No | The factory programs' settings aren't in the manual, only their names |

## Values the manual gives

Every parameter's name, range and (for effects) initial value is the manual's. Effect speeds of 0.03-30 Hz
are kept as 100 steps between those ends, so an initial value such as 0.33 Hz shows as the nearest step (0.32).

## Values set by ear

The manual gives only the 0..127 numbers, not what they mean. These are ours:

| What | Here |
| --- | --- |
| EG and LFO delay/fade times (0..127) | 0, then 2 ms to 20 s, exponentially |
| LFO frequency (0..127) | 0.03 to 30 Hz, exponentially (the same span as the effects' speeds) |
| VDF cutoff (0..127) | 16 Hz to 20 kHz, exponentially |
| VDF | A two-pole low-pass; Color lifts it at the cutoff (Q 0.6 to 2): the manual says it boosts the cutoff region, mildly, and Sound On Sound's review of the NS5R says the filter isn't resonant |
| Pitch LFO intensity | 127 = a semitone |
| Pitch EG intensity | 127 = an octave |
| VDF EG, LFO, wheel and aftertouch amounts | 127 = the cutoff's full range (EG), 48 steps (LFO), 64 steps (wheels, aftertouch) |
| Keyboard tracking | VDF: 127 follows the keyboard; VDA: 127 = 6 dB an octave |
| Velocity on EG times | Up to 4 times shorter or longer |
| VDA level | Its EG squared (a dB-like curve) |
| Effects: every type's sound | Reverbs: an 8-line feedback delay network with early reflections; 09 SPRING REVERB uses TF Spring Reverb's simulated springs; delays, choruses, flangers, phasers, the rotary speaker (slow 0.8 Hz, fast 6.7 Hz), distortion with its wah, exciter, enhancer, EQ shelves at 200 Hz and 5 kHz, the parametric EQ's frequency tables, the resonance filter's sweep |
| Arpeggiator patterns ARP 1-6, B-TECHNO .. B-JAZZ, D-TECHNO .. D-R&B | Made up to suit their names |
| Levels | A voice at full level: −15 dBFS before the effects |

## Not here, from the effects pages

The manual notes that some modulation effects can't be used in both units at once; that limit isn't kept.
